    Learn the indicators

    RSI: Relative Strength Index

    Measures recent gains against recent losses on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 is usually called overbought and below 30 oversold. Between 50 and 70 suggests healthy upward momentum.

    MACD and signal line

    MACD is the gap between the 12- and 26-day EMAs, and the signal line is its 9-day EMA. MACD crossing above the signal line points to improving momentum, and the histogram shows how strong that move is.

    EMA 20 / 50 and SMA 200

    Moving averages smooth out price. EMA 20 above EMA 50 means the short-term trend is up. Price above the 200-day SMA is widely read as a long-term uptrend.

    Bollinger Bands and %B

    Bands two standard deviations above and below the 20-day average. %B shows where the price sits inside them: 1 is the upper band and 0 the lower band. Narrow bands often come before a big move.

    ADX: trend strength

    ADX shows how strong a trend is, not which way it is going. Below 20 suggests a range-bound market and above 25 a trending one. +DI and −DI show which side is in control.

    Stochastic oscillator

    Shows where today's close sits within the last 14 days' range. Above 80 is near the top of the range and below 20 near the bottom.

    ATR and the risk plan

    Average True Range is the typical daily move in rupees. Placing a stop about 1.5× ATR away keeps normal day-to-day noise from triggering it. Size your position so that a stop-out is a loss you can accept.

    Pivot points, support and resistance

    Pivots (P, R1/R2, S1/S2) come from the previous day's high, low and close and are popular intraday reference levels. Swing levels are the highest high and lowest low of the last 20 sessions.
