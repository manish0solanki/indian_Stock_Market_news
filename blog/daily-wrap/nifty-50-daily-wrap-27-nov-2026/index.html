📝 Market Wrap

The Nifty 50 tumbled 1.07% to 25,607.2 on Thursday, shedding 277.3 points as heavy losses in technology and financial stocks masked a resilient performance in the broader market. While the benchmark index struggled, the Nifty Midcap 50 managed a flat close, inching up 0.01% to 17,886.9, suggesting that the selling pressure was largely concentrated in large-cap heavyweights rather than spreading across the board.

The divergence in market breadth tells the real story of the session. Within the Nifty 50, the mood was decidedly grim with 40 stocks declining against just 10 advances. However, the wider NSE market painted a different picture, where 1,535 stocks advanced compared to 1,064 declines. For a retail investor, this contrast is crucial; it indicates that despite the headline index falling sharply, there were actually more buyers than sellers in the broader market, pointing to underlying strength in smaller companies even as blue chips faltered.

Sectorally, IT bore the brunt of the sell-off, sliding 1.88%, dragged down by HCL Technologies, which fell 3.03%, Infosys, down 2.68%, and TCS, which slipped 2.57%. Financial services also weighed heavily, dropping 1.33%, with Shriram Finance emerging as the top loser after plunging 3.99%. Auto stocks faced heat too, with the sector down 1.48% and Bajaj Auto losing 3.36%. On the flip side, metals provided a silver lining, rallying 1.4%, supported by gains in JSW Steel and Hindalco. Bharat Electronics led the gainers' list, rising 1.72%.

Looking ahead, investors will be watching whether the positive breadth in the broader market can sustain if heavyweight selling continues. The key data points to monitor are the Nifty’s ability to hold current levels and whether the IT and financial sectors show signs of stabilising, as their weightage makes them critical for any index recovery.
