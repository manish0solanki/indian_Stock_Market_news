🏦 Flow Analysis

Foreign investors delivered their strongest buying in recent sessions, pouring ₹14,974.68 Cr into Indian equities even as domestic institutions extended their selling streak.

The FII figure more than doubled the previous session’s ₹7,284.03 Cr net purchase, marking a fifth consecutive day of buying. Gross purchases stood at ₹31,797.86 Cr against sales of ₹16,823.18 Cr. Domestic institutions moved in the opposite direction, offloading a net ₹10,444.54 Cr, their fifth straight session of selling and far heavier than the ₹3,712.23 Cr net sale a day earlier. Their gross sales of ₹32,612.82 Cr exceeded purchases of ₹22,168.28 Cr. Together, the two groups still left the cash market with a combined net inflow of ₹4,530.14 Cr.

The Nifty 50 rallied 2.4% to close at 24,536.6, with foreign demand outweighing domestic supply. Over the last five sessions, FIIs have injected a net ₹35,808.74 Cr, while DIIs have withdrawn ₹19,045.33 Cr. For retail investors, this divergence signals that overseas flows are currently doing the heavy lifting, while domestic institutions are using the market strength to reduce exposure. Provisional cash-market data captures institutional activity, not the participation of every investor category.

The next sessions will show whether FII buying can sustain its recent pace and whether DII selling begins to narrow. Watch the combined net flow alongside the Nifty’s move: a widening gap between strong foreign inflows and persistent domestic outflows would keep institutions on opposite sides of the trade.
