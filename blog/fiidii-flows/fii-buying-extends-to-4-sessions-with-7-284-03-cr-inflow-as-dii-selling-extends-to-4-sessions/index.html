🏦 Flow Analysis

Foreign investors extended their buying streak to a fourth session on 4 Nov 2026, pouring a net ₹7284.03 Cr into Indian equities and comfortably absorbing another round of domestic selling.

FIIs bought shares worth ₹21607.38 Cr and sold ₹14323.35 Cr, while DIIs moved the other way, purchasing ₹16392.82 Cr against sales of ₹20105.05 Cr for a net outflow of ₹3712.23 Cr. That also marked a fourth straight session of DII selling. The FII inflow was slightly larger than the ₹6836.81 Cr recorded on 03-Nov-2026, when DIIs were net sellers of ₹3678.85 Cr, so the tug-of-war between the two camps continued with a little more force on both sides. Together, institutions still added a net ₹3571.8 Cr to the cash market.

The Nifty 50 closed at 23960.6, up 0.63%, suggesting overseas demand more than offset domestic profit-taking in the provisional NSE data. Over the last five sessions, FII net buying totalled ₹19890.46 Cr, against DII net selling of ₹6676.38 Cr, underlining how persistent the recent pattern has been. For retail investors, the split matters because headline institutional flows can look modest even when large opposing trades sit underneath; the combined net figure captures only the balance, not the scale of activity on either side.

Next, watch whether the FII buying streak continues, whether DII selling stretches further, and whether the Nifty can build on its 0.63% rise while the two institutional camps remain on opposite sides of the market.
