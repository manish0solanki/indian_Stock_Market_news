🏦 Flow Analysis

Foreign investors returned to the Indian cash market with force on 9 Nov 2026, but domestic institutions almost completely offset their buying, leaving the Nifty 50 with only a modest gain.

FIIs bought shares worth ₹20,310.99 Cr and sold ₹13,098.77 Cr, resulting in net purchases of ₹7,212.22 Cr. This marked one day of buying after the previous session’s marginal net sale of ₹0.25 Cr. DIIs moved in the opposite direction, purchasing ₹12,764.97 Cr and selling ₹19,937.50 Cr, for a net outflow of ₹7,172.53 Cr. Their selling streak extended to eight sessions. The near-balance between the two camps left combined institutional net buying at just ₹39.69 Cr, a fraction of the gross activity on either side.

The wider trend shows how unusual that stand-off is. Across the last five sessions, FIIs have supplied net inflows of ₹43,220.50 Cr, while DIIs have recorded net outflows of ₹27,998.31 Cr. Foreign money has therefore been the larger net support, even as domestic institutions have consistently taken the other side. For retail investors, a positive FII figure means their purchases exceeded sales in the cash market; it does not by itself show which shares rose or whether the broader market participated.

The Nifty 50 closed at 25,044.65, up 0.23%, extending the sense of a quiet index move despite heavy two-way institutional trading. Next, watch whether FII buying persists, whether the eight-session DII selling streak breaks, and if combined flows remain close to flat while the index holds near this close.
