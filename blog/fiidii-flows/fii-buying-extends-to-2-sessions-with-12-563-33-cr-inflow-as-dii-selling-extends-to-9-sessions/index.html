Foreign investors delivered their strongest buying punch in recent sessions, pouring ₹12,563.33 Cr into Indian equities even as domestic institutions extended their selling streak to nine days. The Nifty 50 rallied 0.84% to close at 25,255.05, shrugging off the persistent domestic outflows.

The scale of foreign buying stood out sharply. FII net purchases nearly doubled from the previous session's ₹7,212.22 Cr, with gross buying of ₹30,087.21 Cr overwhelming sales of ₹17,523.88 Cr. This marked their second consecutive day of net buying. Domestic institutions moved in the opposite direction, offloading a net ₹8,434.53 Cr through sales of ₹28,138.92 Cr against purchases of ₹19,704.39 Cr. Their selling intensified from ₹7,172.53 Cr in the prior session.

The combined net inflow of ₹4,128.80 Cr tells the real story: foreign money more than absorbed domestic selling pressure. Over the last five sessions, this divergence has been stark, with FIIs injecting ₹48,499.80 Cr while DIIs pulled out ₹32,720.61 Cr. For retail investors, this pattern signals a market increasingly driven by overseas capital, where index gains rest on foreign appetite rather than broad domestic institutional support.

Watch whether the FII buying streak extends beyond two days and if the pace of DII selling slows. The gap between foreign inflows and domestic outflows remains the key metric, along with whether the Nifty holds above 25,255.05 as these opposing forces battle for control.
