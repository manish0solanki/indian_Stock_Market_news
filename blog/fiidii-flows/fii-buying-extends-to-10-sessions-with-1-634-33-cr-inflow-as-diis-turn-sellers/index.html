🏦 Flow Analysis

Foreign investors extended their buying streak to ten sessions on 27 Nov 2026, but the Nifty 50 still slipped 1.07% to close at 25607.2, showing that institutional support did not translate into index gains.

FIIs bought shares worth ₹14259.45 Cr and sold ₹12625.12 Cr, leaving net purchases of ₹1634.33 Cr. That was well below their ₹5843.33 Cr net buying in the previous session on 26-Nov-2026. DIIs moved in the opposite direction, recording net sales of ₹556.42 Cr after buying ₹5630.72 Cr a day earlier. Their gross purchases stood at ₹17263.65 Cr against sales of ₹17820.07 Cr, marking one session of net selling. Together, the two groups added a net ₹1077.91 Cr to the cash market, according to NSE provisional data.

The contrast matters for retail investors. Positive combined flows mean institutions collectively put more money into shares than they took out, but they do not guarantee a rising benchmark. The Nifty’s decline alongside net institutional buying suggests the flow figures alone cannot explain the day’s market movement. Over the last five sessions, FIIs have been the stronger force, with net buying totalling ₹27938.15 Cr, compared with ₹1473.1 Cr from DIIs.

The next data points to watch are whether FII buying continues after ten sessions, whether DII selling extends beyond one day, and how the Nifty behaves around its 25607.2 close. The gap between institutional flows and index direction will remain central to reading market strength.
