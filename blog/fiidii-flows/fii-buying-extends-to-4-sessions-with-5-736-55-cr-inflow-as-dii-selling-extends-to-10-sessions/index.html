🏦 Flow Analysis

Foreign funds extended their buying spree on 13 Nov 2026, cushioning the Nifty 50’s 0.61% slide to 25,144.30 as domestic institutions kept selling. FIIs purchased shares worth ₹18,339.57 Cr and sold ₹12,603.02 Cr, leaving a net inflow of ₹5,736.55 Cr. This marked their fourth straight session of net buying, though the figure was smaller than the ₹7,830.60 Cr recorded on 12-Nov-2026.

DIIs moved the other way. They bought ₹11,745.95 Cr and sold ₹13,999.53 Cr, producing a net outflow of ₹2,253.58 Cr and stretching their selling streak to 10 sessions. Even so, the latest DII outflow was much narrower than the previous session’s ₹6,331.17 Cr. Together, the two groups still added ₹3,482.97 Cr net to the cash market.

The split matters for ordinary investors because institutional flows show who was providing demand, not a guarantee of where prices will go next. Here, foreign buying outweighed domestic selling, yet the Nifty still slipped. That means positive combined flows did not translate into a higher index close. Over the last five sessions, FIIs have supplied ₹33,342.45 Cr of net buying, while DIIs have recorded ₹24,892.12 Cr of net selling, underlining how persistent the tug-of-war has been.

Next, watch whether the FII buying streak continues and whether DII outflows keep shrinking after the sharp moderation from the previous session. The Nifty’s close at 25,144.30 and its 0.61% fall provide the immediate market reference, while the combined net figure will show whether institutional demand remains positive despite index weakness.
