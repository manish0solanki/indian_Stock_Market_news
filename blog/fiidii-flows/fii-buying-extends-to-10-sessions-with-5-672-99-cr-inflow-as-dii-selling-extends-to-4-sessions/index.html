🏦 Flow Analysis

Foreign funds kept buying Indian shares even as the Nifty 50 slid 1.75% to 25,349.65, extending a stark split between institutional flows and the market’s direction.

FIIs bought ₹23,473.67 Cr and sold ₹17,800.68 Cr in the cash market, leaving net purchases of ₹5,672.99 Cr. That marked a tenth straight session of buying, although the net figure was well below the ₹10,308.42 Cr recorded on 20-Nov-2026. Over the last five sessions, their net buying totalled ₹33,454.98 Cr. DIIs moved the other way, selling a net ₹2,760.12 Cr as purchases of ₹16,505.75 Cr lagged sales of ₹19,265.87 Cr. Their selling streak reached four sessions, but the latest outflow was smaller than the previous session’s ₹4,859.8 Cr. Their five-session net sales stood at ₹11,939.46 Cr.

Together, the two groups still added a net ₹2,912.87 Cr to the cash market. For retail investors, that means foreign buying more than absorbed domestic selling, yet it did not prevent a sharp index decline. Net institutional flow is the difference between purchases and sales, not a guarantee that share prices will rise. The Nifty’s fall alongside positive combined flows underlines that distinction.

The next data points to watch are whether FII buying remains positive after its recent step-down, whether the DII selling streak continues, and if combined flows stay above zero. The Nifty’s movement relative to 25,349.65 will show whether the gap between institutional support and index performance persists. All flow figures are NSE provisional cash-market data.
