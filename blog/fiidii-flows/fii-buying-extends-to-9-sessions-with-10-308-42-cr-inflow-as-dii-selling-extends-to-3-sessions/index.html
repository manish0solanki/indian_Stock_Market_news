🏦 Flow Analysis

Foreign investors extended their buying streak to a ninth session on 20 Nov 2026, pouring in a net ₹10,308.42 Cr even as domestic institutions sold for a third straight day. That overseas demand outweighed the selling and helped the Nifty 50 close at 25,800.6, up 1.12%.

The foreign flow was also stronger than in the previous session. On 19-Nov-2026, FIIs were net buyers of ₹7,411.41 Cr, so the latest figure marks a step-up rather than a fading trend. Their gross purchases stood at ₹23,596.4 Cr against sales of ₹13,287.98 Cr. DIIs moved the other way, buying ₹18,492.63 Cr and selling ₹23,352.43 Cr, leaving a net outflow of ₹4,859.8 Cr. Their selling was much heavier than the ₹1,356.57 Cr net outflow recorded a session earlier.

Together, the two groups still added ₹5,448.62 Cr to the cash market, according to NSE provisional data. The wider pattern is similarly lopsided: across the last five sessions, FIIs have contributed a combined net ₹32,646.67 Cr, while DIIs have withdrawn ₹12,698.39 Cr. For an ordinary investor, that means the latest institutional support has come from overseas flows, not from both sides buying together. A positive combined figure alongside DII selling shows foreign demand absorbing that supply, rather than a uniform institutional stance.

The next data points to watch are whether the FII buying streak continues, whether DII net selling narrows or deepens, and whether combined flows remain positive. The Nifty’s 1.12% rise and 25,800.6 close provide the reference for comparing subsequent index moves with those flows.
