🏦 Flow Analysis

Foreign investors kept Indian equities underpinned on 17 Nov 2026, extending their buying streak to six sessions even as domestic institutions offered only marginal support. FIIs bought shares worth ₹19,930.98 Cr and sold ₹15,735.60 Cr, leaving net purchases of ₹4,195.38 Cr. DIIs ended just ₹196.93 Cr positive, with purchases of ₹14,903.80 Cr narrowly exceeding sales of ₹14,706.87 Cr. Together, the two groups injected ₹4,392.31 Cr into the cash market.

The composition of that support shifted from the previous session. On 16-Nov-2026, FIIs had been stronger net buyers at ₹4,864.68 Cr, while DIIs had pulled out ₹3,519.05 Cr. The latest figures therefore show slightly smaller foreign inflows, but no opposing domestic selling. Over the last five sessions, FII net buying totals ₹35,190.54 Cr, against net DII selling of ₹20,341.40 Cr, underlining how heavily recent institutional support has depended on overseas investors.

The Nifty 50 closed at 25,387.40, up 0.38%. For retail investors, the key distinction is between the index’s modest rise and the flow mix behind it: a positive close shows the benchmark gained, but it does not by itself indicate broad participation across stocks. Provisional cash-market figures also describe institutional activity, not the reason for the move or its durability.

Next, watch whether the FII buying streak continues and whether DIIs build on their single-session return to net buying. The gap between five-session foreign inflows and domestic outflows remains central, alongside whether the Nifty can hold above its latest close. Any renewed divergence between foreign support and domestic selling would matter as much as the headline index change.
