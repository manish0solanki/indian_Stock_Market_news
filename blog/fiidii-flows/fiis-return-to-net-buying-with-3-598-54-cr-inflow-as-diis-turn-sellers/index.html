🏦 Flow Analysis

Foreign investors swung back to buying Indian shares on 30 Oct 2026, while domestic institutions moved the other way.

FIIs purchased ₹20,564.02 Cr and sold ₹16,965.48 Cr in the NSE cash market, leaving net buying of ₹3,598.54 Cr. That reversed their ₹943.60 Cr net outflow in the previous session on 29 Oct 2026. DIIs, meanwhile, bought ₹16,643.55 Cr and sold ₹17,461.06 Cr, resulting in net selling of ₹817.51 Cr. Their stance also flipped from the prior session, when they were net buyers to the tune of ₹1,924.41 Cr. Both flow streaks therefore stand at just one day: FII buying and DII selling.

Together, the two groups pumped in a net ₹2,781.03 Cr. The Nifty 50 closed at 23,053.10, up 0.51%, so the benchmark rose on a day when overseas inflows more than offset domestic selling. For retail investors, the key point is that net flow figures show the balance between institutional purchases and sales, not the full scale of trading. Gross activity was much larger on both sides, and the session’s positive close came alongside opposing FII and DII positions rather than broad agreement between them.

The latest session also fits into a stronger five-session picture: FIIs have total net buying of ₹6,304.67 Cr, while DIIs have net buying of ₹3,109.79 Cr.
