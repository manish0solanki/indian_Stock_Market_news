🏦 Flow Analysis

Foreign investors powered Indian equities higher on 6 Nov 2026, extending their buying streak even as domestic institutions continued to take money off the table.

FIIs bought shares worth ₹30,455.63 Cr and sold ₹16,705.81 Cr, leaving net purchases of ₹13,749.82 Cr. That marked a sixth straight session of buying, though the net figure was smaller than the ₹14,974.68 Cr recorded on 05-Nov-2026. DIIs moved in the opposite direction, with purchases of ₹18,084.32 Cr against sales of ₹24,053.02 Cr, resulting in net sales of ₹5,968.70 Cr. Their selling streak also stretched to six sessions, but the latest net outflow was narrower than the previous session’s ₹10,444.54 Cr. Together, the two groups still added ₹7,781.12 Cr net to the cash market.

The Nifty 50 closed at 25,030.35, up 2.01%, showing that the market rallied alongside strong combined institutional support. For retail investors, the key distinction is between headline index strength and the source of that support: foreign inflows remained the dominant force, while domestic institutions were net sellers. Over the last five sessions, FIIs have invested a net ₹45,960.02 Cr, compared with net DII sales of ₹24,196.52 Cr.

The next data points to watch are whether FII buying remains broad and sustained, whether DII selling continues to shrink, and if combined net inflows stay positive. The Nifty’s move around 25,030.35 and the persistence of both six-session streaks will help show whether this pattern is holding.
