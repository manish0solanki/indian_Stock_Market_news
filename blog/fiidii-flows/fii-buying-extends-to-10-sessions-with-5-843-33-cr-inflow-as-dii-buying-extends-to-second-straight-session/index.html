🏦 Flow Analysis

Foreign funds extended their buying spree to a tenth straight session on 26 Nov 2026, and domestic institutions joined in, giving the Nifty 50 a modest lift.

Foreign institutional investors bought shares worth ₹17,879.74 Cr and sold ₹12,036.41 Cr, leaving net purchases of ₹5,843.33 Cr. That was stronger than the previous session’s ₹4,479.08 Cr on 25-Nov-2026. Domestic institutional investors were almost as active, with net buying of ₹5,630.72 Cr from purchases of ₹24,352.87 Cr and sales of ₹18,722.15 Cr. Their buying streak reached two sessions, after a net inflow of ₹4,018.72 Cr in the previous session. Together, the two groups pumped a net ₹11,474.05 Cr into the cash market, according to NSE provisional data.

The Nifty 50 closed at 25,884.5, up 0.25%. In plain terms, the benchmark rose only slightly even though institutional money flowed in strongly; net flow figures show the balance of buying over selling, not a guarantee that every stock advanced. The longer view also changes the picture. Over the last five sessions, FIIs supplied a combined net ₹33,715.23 Cr, while DIIs added just ₹672.95 Cr. That makes the latest domestic contribution stand out against its recent five-session total, while foreign buying remains the larger force across that stretch.

For the next sessions, the data points to watch are whether the FII streak continues, whether DII net buying stays closer to the latest session’s pace, and whether the combined flow remains above the previous session’s levels. The Nifty’s close at 25,884.5 and its 0.25% gain provide the immediate reference for judging whether similarly large flows coincide with a stronger or weaker index move.
