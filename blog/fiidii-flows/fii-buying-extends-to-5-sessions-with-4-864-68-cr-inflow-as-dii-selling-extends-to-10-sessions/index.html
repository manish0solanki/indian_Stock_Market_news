🏦 Flow Analysis

Foreign investors did the heavy lifting on Dalal Street, extending their buying streak even as domestic institutions pulled money out for a tenth straight session. On 16 Nov 2026, FIIs bought shares worth ₹15,636.88 Cr and sold ₹10,772.2 Cr, leaving net purchases of ₹4,864.68 Cr, according to NSE provisional cash-market data. DIIs moved the other way, with purchases of ₹11,752.03 Cr against sales of ₹15,271.08 Cr, for a net outflow of ₹3,519.05 Cr.

The foreign bid was smaller than in the previous session on 13-Nov-2026, when FII net buying stood at ₹5,736.55 Cr, but it still marked five consecutive days of net buying. Domestic selling, by contrast, intensified: the DII net figure was ₹-2,253.58 Cr in the prior session. Together, the two camps left a combined net inflow of ₹1,345.63 Cr, showing that foreign demand more than absorbed domestic supply.

The Nifty 50 closed at 25,291.1, up 0.58%. For retail investors, the flow split matters because a rising index alongside persistent DII selling points to foreign institutions providing the marginal support, rather than both groups buying together. The wider pattern underlines that divide: across the last five sessions, FII net buying totalled ₹38,207.38 Cr, while DII net selling reached ₹27,710.86 Cr.

Next, watch whether the FII buying streak continues at a similar pace, whether the DII selling streak stretches beyond 10 sessions, and whether the combined net flow stays positive. The Nifty’s ability to hold near 25,291.1 while that tug-of-war continues will be the key data point to track.
