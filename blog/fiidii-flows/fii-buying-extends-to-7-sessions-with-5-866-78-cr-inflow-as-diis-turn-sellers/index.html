🏦 Flow Analysis

Foreign investors stepped up their cash-market buying on 18 Nov 2026 even as the Nifty 50 slipped, leaving domestic institutions to do the selling.

FIIs bought shares worth ₹17,913.16 Cr and sold ₹12,046.38 Cr, producing net purchases of ₹5,866.78 Cr. That extended their buying streak to seven sessions and was stronger than the previous session’s ₹4,195.38 Cr net inflow on 17-Nov-2026. DIIs moved in the opposite direction: purchases of ₹14,602.25 Cr were outweighed by sales of ₹17,762.15 Cr, giving a net outflow of ₹3,159.9 Cr and a one-day selling streak. They had been net buyers of ₹196.93 Cr in the previous session.

Together, the two groups still added ₹2,706.88 Cr to the cash market. Yet the Nifty 50 closed at 25,227.95, down 0.63%. For retail investors, that split is the key detail: positive institutional flow does not automatically mean a rising index. The provisional NSE figures show who bought and sold more than they traded, not which shares gained or why the market fell.

The wider pattern is also lopsided. Across the last five sessions, FIIs have supplied net inflows of ₹28,493.99 Cr, while DIIs have recorded net outflows of ₹15,066.77 Cr. Foreign buying has therefore done the heavier lifting, with domestic institutions partly offsetting it.

Next, watch whether the FII buying streak continues, whether DII selling persists beyond one session, and whether combined flows remain positive. The Nifty’s move after this 0.63% decline will show whether institutional support is being matched by broader market strength.
