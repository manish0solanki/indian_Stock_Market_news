🏦 Flow Analysis

Foreign funds kept the market’s engine running on 2 Nov 2026, pouring in enough cash to lift the Nifty 50 even as domestic institutions pulled back.

FIIs bought shares worth ₹16,499.46 Cr and sold ₹13,384.78 Cr, leaving a net inflow of ₹3,114.68 Cr. That extended their buying streak to two sessions, though the net figure was smaller than the ₹3,598.54 Cr recorded on 30-Oct-2026. DIIs moved the other way, with purchases of ₹14,988.48 Cr against sales of ₹15,380.68 Cr, producing a net outflow of ₹392.2 Cr and a second straight session of selling. Their selling was also milder than the previous session’s ₹817.51 Cr net outflow.

Together, the two groups added ₹2,722.48 Cr to the cash market. The Nifty 50 closed at 23,440.8, up 1.68%, a strong move that coincided with continued foreign buying. Over the last five sessions, FIIs have supplied net inflows of ₹10,881.84 Cr, while DIIs have contributed just ₹108.02 Cr on a net basis, underlining how lopsided institutional support has been. For retail investors, that gap matters: a rising index backed mainly by one group can look powerful, but the flow data shows domestic institutions have not been matching that enthusiasm.

The next sessions’ provisional NSE figures will show whether FII buying remains broad and persistent, whether DII selling fades after two sessions, and whether the combined net flow stays positive. The Nifty’s ability to hold near 23,440.8 after a 1.68% rise is the other data point to track.
